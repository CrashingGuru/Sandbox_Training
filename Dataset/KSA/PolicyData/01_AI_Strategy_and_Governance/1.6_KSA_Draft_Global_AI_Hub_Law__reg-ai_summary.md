## Abstract

Draft law that would create a Saudi legal framework attracting **foreign governments and private-sector entities** to host data + AI workloads in Saudi Arabia under a new "data-embassy" model. Introduces three sovereign-hub hosting models — **Private, Extended, and Virtual** — each with different jurisdictional rules, licensing and security requirements. The distinctive feature is that **foreign entities can host data in KSA under their own home-jurisdiction law**, provided they meet CST registration, licensing, and NCA-aligned cybersecurity controls. Positions KSA as a regional data-sovereignty hub in the same spirit as Estonia's data-embassy pact.

## Key provisions

- **Data Embassy model** — private data centres dedicated to a foreign entity's data/apps under **foreign** legal jurisdiction (a Saudi-borders exception)
- **Three hub models**:
  - **Private** — dedicated to one foreign government / entity
  - **Extended** — Saudi CSP operating under a foreign entity's law + oversight
  - **Virtual** — cross-border cloud-service with a Saudi legal anchor
- **Cooperation triangle** — CST + SDAIA + NCA jointly govern registration, cybersecurity, and AI-workload compliance
- **Security floor** — NCA ECC-2:2024 + CCC-1:2020 + DCC-1:2022 apply irrespective of foreign law choice
- **Extraterritoriality** — the first substantive law in the region that creates a partial extraterritorial-jurisdiction zone inside a sovereign state

## Why this matters for our notebook rows

- **Row 9 (Federated learning)** — Data Embassy model provides an *alternative* to federation for cross-jurisdictional collaboration; teams can compare federated-in-country vs data-embassy patterns.
- **Row 10 (Agentic AI on sovereign compute)** — the AI Hub Law is the legal instrument that lets foreign AI vendors host workloads in KSA under home-country IP + liability regimes.
- **Row 4 (5G / beam forecasting)** — CST is the promulgating body; the same regulator that owns spectrum policy owns the Hub Law.
