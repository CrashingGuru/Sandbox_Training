## Abstract

Overview of Saudi Arabia's AI-regulation stack as it stands in 2026. Positions three anchor institutions — **SDAIA** (AI + data + PDPL), **NCA** (cybersecurity), and **CST** (telecom + space + cloud + AI Hub Law) — as the operating triumvirate. Explains how the **Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Royal Decree M/19 of 2021 as amended by M/148 of 2023)** and the **National Cybersecurity Framework** form the two horizontal legal bases, while **SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles v2 (2023) + Generative AI Guidelines (2024) + AI Adoption Framework (2024)** provide the AI-specific operating rules. Also flags the two upcoming legal developments: (i) a new **Copyright Law (Royal Decree, published 13 February 2026, in force 1 August 2026)** that explicitly permits reproduction of original works for AI training without author consent or compensation, and (ii) the **Draft Global AI Hub Law** (CST, April 2025 public consultation).

## The Feb 2026 Copyright Law carveout — significant

The new KSA Copyright Law (published 13 Feb 2026, in force 1 Aug 2026) explicitly **permits reproduction of original works for developing AI products without author permission or compensation**. This is a materially different position from EU's TDM opt-out or the US fair-use case-law. Directly relevant to Row 10 (LLM RAG on regulatory PDFs) — Saudi jurisdiction gives you a permissive training-data regime that other jurisdictions do not.
